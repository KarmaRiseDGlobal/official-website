# Alma: founder profile and brand voice

Canonical reference for Alma's background, operational role, cultural and ethical touchstones, Peninsular Spanish and English editorial guardrails, and reusable `VOICE PROFILE` across KarmaRise D Global, AlmaRise D Brand, and the Women's Training Initiative.

## Who Alma is

- **Role:** Co-founder of KarmaRise D Global and namesake founder of AlmaRise D Brand alongside Shirish. Spanish entrepreneur with prior experience running her own companies and helping international clients import from Asia (including China). Certified English teacher as a foreign language who speaks Spanish, English, and Portuguese.
- **Operational lane:** Serves as the direct client-facing bridge in Spain and the rest of Europe while travelling back and forth to work on the ground in India. Runs initial client video calls, sends written meeting conclusions (`conclusiones de la reunión`) immediately afterward, conducts retail fieldwork in Spain (Madrid, Donostia), reviews physical samples before clients approve them, audits family-run production units and women artisan collectives in person across India (including specialist leather and craft hubs in Kolkata, Kanpur, Agra, and Chennai), and directs product ergonomics, garment versatility, KarmaRise's four manufacturing categories (leather bags and other leather goods, clothing in natural and varied fabrics, custom sewn accessories, and home decor), and AlmaRise D Brand's lines (`AlmaRise D Reborn`, `AlmaRise D Organic`, and `AlmaRise D Home Decor`).
- **Origin with Shirish and India:** First visited India (Pushkar, Rajasthan) in January 2023 through a friend's small NGO after already volunteering in Spain with single-mother immigrant housing and job training. Met Shirish after the COVID lockdown at an online international language conference connected to Birla College; when he reached out asking her to help with his English, their ongoing conversations on Indian history, culture, and women's livelihoods evolved into KarmaRise D Global and AlmaRise D Brand.
- **How she works with clients and production units:** Insists on face-to-face video calls ("we have face and eyes, not a machine behind a screen"), written confirmation of every point (warmly framed as `conclusiones de la reunión` / clear meeting notes rather than stiff `acta` in web copy), explicit NDA confidentiality, guiding clients on what is needed for each dye batch and creating physical colour swatches for client approval rather than relying on phone photos ("tan is a very vague concept"), reviewing samples first and flagging even tiny refinements openly before production proceeds, and spending full days on the ground talking directly with workers rather than only with facility owners. Walks away from any production unit that stays secretive after initial caution.
- **Public editorial guardrails:**
  - Never name confidential clients publicly (do not print Snoby).
  - Never print Ulhasnagar in public editorial, marketing, or UI copy (reserve strictly for legal and postal `<address>` disclosures of `Karmarise Global LLP`; use "our studio in India", "our office in India", or "near Mumbai").
  - Spell the owned label **`AlmaRise D Brand`** (and associated lines **`AlmaRise D Reborn`**, **`AlmaRise D Organic`**, **`AlmaRise D Home Decor`**) in all visible copy, keeping existing URL slugs (`/almarise`) lowercase.
  - Mention the languages Alma speaks (Spanish, English, Portuguese) **only where relevant or helpful**, such as Q&A/FAQs, call-booking pages, or contact contexts, never as boilerplate across every Hero or general section.
  - Do not portray Alma as a local Indian encyclopaedia; she observes India honestly as a Spaniard living and working there who keeps learning.
  - Keep Peninsular Spanish (`es-ES`) native, warm, and idiomatic (`de tú a tú`), never mechanically translated from English. Never use English UI calques (`prerrellenar`, `deja tu categoría preseleccionada`, `pistas de mostrador`, `vender directo al escaparate`, `capas` for everyday garments). Between `piel` and `cuero`, use whichever feels most natural in context.
  - Never write `España y Europa` / `Spain and Europe` as if Spain were outside Europe; use `España y el resto de Europa` (`Spain and the rest of Europe`) or `España o en algún lugar de Europa`.
  - Avoid `workshop floor`, `sales floor`, `a pie de taller`, `bulk cutting`, `cortar la producción`, `filtrar muestras`, technical `tejido plano` in general marketing copy, and repetitive `workshops` / `talleres`; use operational facility terms (`production sites`, `production units`, `manufacturing units`, `facilities` / `unidades de producción`, `puntos de producción`, `lugares de producción`) alongside craft-specific nouns.
  - Do not add social media links in KarmaRise web copy.

## Core worldview and recurring touchstones

- **Small business owners fighting to keep the lights on:** Pictures European clients as independent entrepreneurs ("grandes emprendedores y emprendedoras") who lift the shutter every morning (`subir el cierre de tu tienda cada día`) while pressured by mass online shopping, needing a partner that lets them offer tailor-made fashion (`ofrecer moda a medida`) rather than anonymous wholesale stock (`comprar a un mayorista`).
- **AlmaRise D Brand (`AlmaRise D Reborn`, `AlmaRise D Organic`, and `AlmaRise D Home Decor`):** Built around giving a second life (`dar una segunda vida`) at every level. `AlmaRise D Reborn` transforms treasured, breathable cotton, linen, and silk saris that Indian women have kept and that carry a story (`saris que mujeres indias han guardado y que tienen una historia que contar`) into versatile everyday garments (`prendas de uso diario`, never `capas`) that revive forgotten outfits in a European woman's wardrobe. `AlmaRise D Organic` works with organic dyes and plant/fruit-waste fabrics (sugarcane, orange, banana, and pineapple, among others / `entre otros`) with natural drape. AlmaRise D Brand also extends beyond those two lines into **AlmaRise D Home Decor** (`decoración del hogar`).
- **Zero exploitation and family-run production units:** Explicitly avoids massive mass-production factories in favour of established family-run production units (`fábricas de corte familiar`, `unidades de producción`, `puntos de producción`) with hand-stitching and pedal machines, plus women artisan collectives (`colectivos de artesanas`) across India. States the ethical boundary plainly: zero exploitation of minors, adults, or women.
- **"Teach to fish," never pity:** Rejects condescending charity ("poor Indian" gives her bad goosebumps) and handout loops (such as the Rajasthan milk-resale scam). Frames the social leg (`la pata social`) around giving women in exclusion a real second chance (`una segunda oportunidad`) through skills and dignified work.
- **A woman's practical eye on product design:** Tests every bag and garment by real daily wear: correct strap drop (`strap drop`, not "strap sitting lower"), smooth high-quality Japanese plastic YKK zippers (`cremalleras japonesas YKK de plástico de alta calidad`, one single spec) that do not scratch hands or ruin winter coats, light cotton lining so items are visible inside, top grab handles on backpacks, comfortable cuts (`corte cómodo`, not `rígido`), and garments that work on Spanish public transport (metro, local train, bus) and move from jeans to a wedding.
- **Everyday India-Spain observation:** Dismantles lazy "Europe vs. India" generalizations by naming real countries, schedules, and kitchens (Spain's bar-and-hospitality rhythm, England's early non-spicy dinners, France's rigid lunch hours, Diwali _chakli_ and _shankarpali_, three biryanis on Eid, and her mum's huge minced-meat _croquetas_ for a family of eight).
- **Lifelong learning and accountability:** Believes there is no age limit for learning, only will. Refuses to overpromise perfection ("I cannot assure nothing will go off plan, because that is not in my hand"), and instead commits to checking everything two, three, five times and putting every decision in writing.
