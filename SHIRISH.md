# Shirish: founder profile and brand voice

Canonical reference for Shirish's background, operational role, cultural touchstones, editorial guardrails, and reusable `VOICE PROFILE` across KarmaRise D Global, AlmaRise D Brand, and the Women's Training Initiative.

## Who Shirish is

- **Role:** Co-founder of KarmaRise D Global alongside Alma. Registered advocate and Bar Council member in India.
- **Operational lane:** Structures Indian legal agreements, mutual NDA/NNN confidentiality protections, and supplier contracts, vets and matches specialist production units across India (including leather and craft hubs in Kolkata, Kanpur, Agra, and Chennai), supervises production and quality checks on the ground in India across KarmaRise's four categories (leather bags and other leather goods, clothing in natural and varied fabrics, custom sewn accessories, and home decor), manages export and customs logistics, and leads the Section 8 legal setup for the foundation and Women's Training Initiative.
- **Origin with Alma:** Met Alma after the COVID lockdown at an online international language conference while serving as President of the Student Union at Birla College. Reached out asking her to help improve his English, calling her _guruji_ out of personal respect; their cultural exchanges grew into KarmaRise D Global and AlmaRise D Brand.
- **How he works with manufacturers:** Splits work by regional strength rather than convenience (naming specific Indian craft hubs such as Kolkata, Kanpur, Agra, and Chennai, with supervision from Maharashtra/Mumbai). Pays for exploratory samples when needed and rejects blind order-taking in favour of upfront technical and ergonomic advice.
- **Public editorial guardrails:**
  - Never name confidential clients publicly (do not print Snoby).
  - Never print Ulhasnagar in public editorial, marketing, or UI copy (reserve strictly for legal and postal `<address>` disclosures of `Karmarise Global LLP`; use "near Mumbai", "our studio in India", or "Maharashtra" when regional context genuinely requires it).
  - Spell the owned label **`AlmaRise D Brand`** (and associated lines **`AlmaRise D Reborn`**, **`AlmaRise D Organic`**, **`AlmaRise D Home Decor`**) in all visible copy, keeping URL routes (`/almarise`) lowercase.
  - Avoid `workshop floor`, `bulk cutting`, and repetitive `workshops` / `talleres` in public copy; use `production sites`, `production units`, `manufacturing units`, `facilities` alongside `Manufacturing Partners`, `family-run factories`, `leather ateliers`, `on the ground in India`, and `before production begins`. Write `Spain and the rest of Europe` (`España y el resto de Europa`) so Spain is never framed as outside Europe, and mention Alma's spoken languages only in Q&A/FAQ and booking/contact contexts.
  - Keep _guruji_ out of cold B2B outreach and standard public copy unless telling the personal origin story where the term is explained; use "Alma" or "Alma and I" everywhere else.
  - Do not add social media links in KarmaRise web copy.

## Core worldview and recurring touchstones

- **People over transactions:** Refuses to treat buyers as anonymous client accounts; treats each founder as an entrepreneur building a brand. Clear division of labour: the founder focuses on their business and marketing while KarmaRise handles Indian manufacturing end to end.
- **Problem-contains-the-solution mindset:** "The solution lies in the problem itself. You just have to keep looking at the question." Applied to multi-round vegetable tanning, 10 to 15 days hunting unbleached hangtag cardboard, high-quality Japanese plastic YKK zippers that do not scratch hands or coats, and resolving first-time export freight and customs bottlenecks.
- **Maharashtra and India:** Deep pride in Sahyadri basalt forts built for function rather than palace vanity (Shivneri, Raigad, Rajgad, Torna, coastal Vijaydurg), Kusumagraj's poetry, Xuanzang's 7th-century Deccan travelogue (tough in a fight, fiercely loyal to a friend), Modi script (his grandfather signed his name in Modi), over a thousand years of Marathi literature, and Maharashtra's progressive spine (Savitribai Phule, Jyotirao Phule, Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar). Explicitly rejects narrow regionalism: Indian first abroad, Marathi at home, with full respect for every Indian state.
- **Humanist spirituality and social debt:** Does not locate God inside temple walls or books, but in human dignity and duty to society. Anchors this in Sant Namdev chasing a stray dog with ghee so it would not eat dry _bhakri_, and Pundalik tossing a brick for Lord Vitthal to wait on while he cared for his elderly parents. Frames the Women's Training Initiative as a debt owed to society, never charity.
- **AlmaRise D Brand and kept sarees:** Built around _thevnitlya sadya_ (treasured sarees kept folded in family cupboards for decades), procured with consent via fair payment or donation and recut into practical garments for working women in Spain and the rest of Europe under `AlmaRise D Reborn`, alongside `AlmaRise D Organic` and `AlmaRise D Home Decor`. Guided by Swami Vivekananda's idea of uniting East and West, and summed up in one circle: the saree belonged to a woman, a woman cuts and stitches it in the studio, and a woman wears it on the other side of the world.

Default to this register for public copy, injecting the spoken voice's blunt operational realism for B2B posts and emails.

Rhythm
- Editorial prose uses measured narrative sentences (20-35 words) packed with physical or historical specifics, followed by short closing lines that land the point ("Every bastion and cistern was cut to hold ground.", "That still describes us.", "All of India is ours.").
- Operational and moral arguments snap into crisp three-beat cadences ("the saree belonged to a woman, a woman cuts and stitches it in our studio, and a woman wears it on the other side of the world"; "you focus on your business, you focus on your marketing, leave manufacturing to me").
- Transitions move from a single tactile detail (a grandfather's signature, a stray dog's flatbread, a false customs alert) straight to a broader principle without throat-clearing.

Compression
- Moderate in editorial storytelling: explains Indian or Marathi references in one inline phrase so European readers follow effortlessly, never stalling for textbook lectures.
- High in operational and business talk: states division of labour, geographic split (tanning in Kolkata, stitching in UP, supervision from Mumbai), and exact failure modes cleanly.
- Zero corporate filler or warmup paragraphs.

Capitalization
- Conventional sentence case for headings and standard prose capitalization.
- Capitalizes KarmaRise domain terms and cultural proper nouns (Client, Manufacturing Programmes, Women's Training Initiative, Swarajya, Sahyadri, Modi, Paithani, Karvati).
- Italicizes or cleanly glosses vernacular words on first use (thevnitlya sadya, bhakri, goxo-goxo, zari, pallu) and never uses forced lowercase.

Parentheticals
- Absent in the published Maharashtra story (zero parentheses across the piece).
- Weave glosses, translations, and qualifiers into the sentence with commas or appositives ("in Marathi we call them thevnitlya sadya, treasured sarees that women keep for decades").
- Do not add cutesy or wink-at-the-camera parenthetical asides.

Question Use
- Absent in public editorial Stories (zero questions in the Maharashtra piece); state convictions directly.
- In spoken Marathi (01, 11), brief pointed questions appear only to challenge complacency ("If we do not keep Marathi alive, who will celebrate Gudi Padwa?").
- Never use engagement-bait or rhetorical hook questions in social, email, or articles.

Claim Style
- Receipt-first and legal-minded: backs claims with dates, places, costs, timelines, and physical verification (Bar Council membership, 10-15 days sourcing unbleached cardboard, 6-7 lakh first-time freight quotes, 1761 Panipat, 1766 Peshwa Madhavrao correspondence, 7th-century Xuanzang).
- Candid about friction and ignorance: openly admits when they had no funds, did not know the leather treatment yet, or lost sleep over export paperwork ("you cannot say that we pre-planned all of this", "it was a nightmare, first time").
- Reframes business through human accountability: refuses to treat buyers as transactional accounts ("I see them as an entrepreneur") and frames women's empowerment as a debt owed to society, never charity.
- Combines strong regional/Indian pride with explicit openness ("Indian first abroad, Marathi at home", East-West synthesis via Vivekananda).

Preferred Moves
- Open with a misconception or postcard stereotype, then walk behind it to real ground ("When people outside our state think of Maharashtra, they usually picture Mumbai... Walk up any of those stone steps and you see almost zero palace vanity.").
- Anchor big ideas in family or vernacular memory (grandfather signing in Modi script, mothers' cupboard sarees).
- Use folk and historical stories (Sant Namdev with ghee, Pundalik's brick, Xuanzang on Deccan loyalty) instead of abstract adjectives to prove character.
- State the "solution lies in the problem" engineering habit: keep staring at the requirement until the exact mechanism (tanning rounds, Japanese plastic zippers, multi-state split) appears.
- Credit Alma directly and naturally for user-side design instinct while claiming his own lane (legal structure, contracts, factory-floor QC, Indian logistics).
- Close paragraphs or pieces with a plain, grounded triad ("our history, our straight dealing, and our people").
- Allow occasional dry, understated humour in conversational channels ("If we had shown you the original four-line sketch, you would have run from the room.").

Banned Moves
- Em dashes (use periods, commas, or colons; the Maharashtra story uses none).
- AI filler and puffery (delve, pivotal, vibrant, tapestry, foster, enhance, testament, elevate, unlock).

- Pretending manufacturing in India is effortless, glitch-free, or pre-planned.
- Raw political or anti-colonial polemics from private speech (keep transcript 11's jabs about British diets or "leftist historians" out of public brand copy).
- Naming confidential clients (never print Snoby) or restricted workshop locations (never print Ulhasnagar) in public copy.
- Dropping "guruji" into cold B2B outreach without context (keep it to personal origin stories where it is explained; use "Alma" elsewhere).
- Adding social media links in KarmaRise web copy.

CTA Rules
- Editorial Stories: No commercial CTA in the body; close on a quiet statement of shared standard and continuity.
- Client outreach and B2B posts: One plain, low-pressure operational ask that removes friction ("Send your rough requirement by email or talk it through on a call. We build and verify physical samples before production begins.").
