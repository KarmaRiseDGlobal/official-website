## Product overview

**One-liner:** KarmaRise D Global develops and delivers own-brand leather goods, clothing, textile accessories, and textile home decor for businesses in Spain and the rest of Europe through hands-on manufacturing across India, from Product Brief to doorstep delivery.

**What it does:** Co-founded by Alma (Spanish business owner working between Spain and India) and Shirish (Indian lawyer and Bar Council member managing Indian operations), KarmaRise D Global acts as one continuous team across both sides of production. Clients in Spain and the rest of Europe share an idea, sketch, or favourite reference piece; Alma and Shirish turn it into a written Product Brief (with mutual NDA availability), match and audit specialist production units, production sites, family-run factories, leather ateliers, garment makers, or women artisan collectives across India's craft hubs (including Kolkata, Kanpur, Agra, and Chennai) in person, guide Clients on what is needed and create physical colour swatches for sign-off, review physical samples before cutting starts, supervise production in person on the ground in India, verify EU labelling, and manage export logistics to the Client's business door. Alongside its core B2B Manufacturing Programmes across four categories (leather goods, clothing, textile accessories, and textile home decor), KarmaRise D Global owns the fashion and lifestyle brand **AlmaRise D Brand** spanning **AlmaRise D Reborn** (one-off everyday garments cut from saris Indian women have kept that carry a story), **AlmaRise D Organic** (garments made with organic dyes and plant/fruit-waste textiles such as sugarcane, orange, banana, and pineapple, among others), and **AlmaRise D Home Decor**, and is preparing the **Women's Training Initiative** in India.

**Product category:** End-to-end own-brand product development and managed manufacturing in India for European retailers and fashion brands across four core categories: (1) leather goods (contemporary hobo bags, stylish everyday backpacks, small crossbodies, pouches, wallets, belts, travel bags), (2) clothing (cotton and linen midi dresses and skirts, unique denim pieces, blouses, jumpsuits, outerwear), (3) textile accessories (pashminas, scarves, shawls, thick canvas/linen and quilted totes, embroidered clutches, toiletry/travel cases, padded headbands, headwraps, scrunchies, fabric belts, kimono/obi sashes, fabric sleeves), and (4) textile home decor (cushion covers, wall hangings, Kantha-style bedspreads, recycled-cotton flat-weave dhurries, table runners, tablecloths, placemats). Secondary brand category (**AlmaRise D Brand**): sustainable fashion and lifestyle spanning `AlmaRise D Reborn` (one-of-a-kind upcycled Indian saree garments), `AlmaRise D Organic` (organic plant/fruit-waste textiles), and `AlmaRise D Home Decor`.

**Product type:** High-touch B2B managed manufacturing service (Manufacturing Programmes) plus owned fashion and lifestyle label (**AlmaRise D Brand**, currently in early showcase/development) operated by Karmarise Global LLP (incorporated in India, 15 May 2025).

**Business model:** Project-based custom proposals scoped per Product Brief (covering development, physical sampling, production-unit manufacturing, on-ground quality oversight, and export delivery). No public pricing, commission structure, or fixed minimum order quantities are published or discussed in marketing materials; every Manufacturing Programme is assessed individually after a discovery call and Product Brief review.

## Target audience

**Target companies:**

- **Primary (B2B Manufacturing Programmes):** Independent retail boutiques (`comercios`), emerging and established fashion and home-decor brands, multi-store retail chains, and entrepreneurs launching their first private-label line in Spain (primary market) and the rest of Europe (including Portugal and neighbouring EU markets).
- **Secondary (AlmaRise D Brand):** Active, self-empowered women in Spain and the rest of Europe looking for breathable, versatile, one-of-a-kind garments (shrugs, kimonos, tunics, dresses) and home decor pieces that bring craft and originality into daily life.
- **Supply-side audience (India):** Well-established, family-run Indian production units (`fábricas de corte familiar`, `unidades de producción`) and women artisan collectives willing to work transparently with open doors.

**Decision-makers:**

- Independent shop owners and retail founders (often deciding alone, "fighting every day to open and turn on the lights of their shop").
- Brand founders and creative directors launching or scaling a private label.
- Purchasing, product development, or merchandising leads at retail chains evaluating India as a production base or looking to improve margin and quality over current suppliers.

**Primary use case:** Turning retail floor observations ("the bag needs a longer strap," "the fabric is right, but the fit is wrong") or an existing product into a custom own-brand Bulk Order manufactured in India without losing control of colour, quality, ergonomics, or delivery timelines.

**Jobs to be done:**

- Launch an own-brand capsule or leather-goods line to differentiate a physical shop from mass online retailers and generic wholesale stock.
- Move an existing product or improve price, quality, and responsiveness compared with current sourcing (including China or opaque intermediaries) without risking payment loss or silent material substitutions.
- Develop a bespoke product from scratch (sketches, rough ideas, or reference items) with a partner who advises proactively on ergonomics, hardware, linings, and EU compliance rather than executing blindly.

**Use cases:**

- **Independent boutique launching signature leather bags:** Redesigning best-selling bag shapes with upgraded hardware (antique brass, high-quality Japanese plastic YKK zippers), lighter weight, light cotton linings, natural dyes, and custom hangtags/dust bags.
- **First-time brand founder without tech packs:** Starting from a 15-minute video call or a 4-line sketch and getting guided through Product Brief specification, material sourcing, and physical sample sign-off.
- **Established brand migrating or diversifying into India:** Shifting an active garment, accessory, leather, or home decor line to specialist Indian craft hubs (Kolkata, Kanpur, Agra, Chennai, with supervision from Maharashtra) with in-person on-site checks.

## Problems and pain points

**Core problem:** Making the leap to manufacture own-brand products in India from Spain or Europe (`pegar el salto a la India`) is opaque, fragmented, and risky for small and mid-sized brands, while mass-market sourcing leaves independent shops selling the same generic goods as everyone else.

**Why alternatives fall short:**

- **Large mass-production factories (India, Bangladesh, Thailand, Sri Lanka):** Built for huge _prêt-à-porter_ volumes and rigid MOQs; ignore small or tailored runs and rarely collaborate on ergonomic or custom refinements.
- **Remote sourcing agents and online B2B directories:** Communicate over email/chat without sitting on the workshop floor; pass along vague briefs, fail to pre-screen bad samples, and disappear when dye batches, stitching, or customs go sideways.
- **Direct DIY factory outreach in India:** Language, cultural, and legal gaps lead to polite "yes" answers that hide missing machinery, overstretched lines, or payment-without-delivery horror stories.
- **Staying with wholesale or existing Chinese suppliers:** Keeps independent retailers trapped selling copy-paste stock that competes directly with online platforms on price, or leaves margins squeezed.

**What it costs them:**

- Wasted months and money on unusable samples (for example, chasing a specific leather colour via phone photos instead of a physical swatch, or paying workshops that lack the right machinery).
- Unsellable inventory or customer complaints from overlooked details (metal zippers scratching hands or pilling winter coats, dark linings hiding contents, wrong bag proportions, or silent substitutions during bulk cutting).
- First-time export and customs shocks (unaccounted air-freight quotes that can exceed production cost, or unclear EU leather/textile labelling rules).
- Missed seasonal launch windows and lost margin to online competition.

**Emotional tension:** Fear of wiring money to an unknown workshop in India and getting cheated or ignored; anxiety during a first order even after months of calls; frustration at being besieged by easy online shopping while fighting daily to keep a physical shop alive; exhaustion from chasing suppliers who say "yes, I do" and fail to deliver.

## Competitive landscape

**Direct:** Independent sourcing agencies and India buying houses - fall short because they often operate as transactional brokers talking only to factory owners, pushing volume over craft fit, rarely offering native Spanish founder-to-founder retail accompaniment, and lacking combined legal contract discipline plus hands-on women's product ergonomics.

**Secondary:** Alibaba / IndiaMART self-serve factory sourcing and Chinese OEM/ODM manufacturers - fall short because China's mass channels favour standardized runs and copy-paste models rather than tactile small-batch customization, while self-serve Indian directories leave European founders exposed to unvetted workshops, vague specs, and zero on-site production oversight.

**Indirect:** Buying ready-made wholesale stock from third-party brands or producing solely in high-cost European workshops - falls short because wholesale removes brand uniqueness and margin control, while small-batch European manufacturing often prices independent shops out of competitive retail margins.

## Differentiation

**Key differentiators:**

- **One continuous founder team across Spain and India:** Alma (Spanish entrepreneur, fluent in Spanish, English, and Portuguese) handles European client alignment and retail ergonomics, while Shirish (Indian lawyer, Bar Council member) structures Indian supplier contracts, audits Manufacturing Partners, and leads in-person production supervision. Both travel and work on the ground in India together.
- **Multi-cluster matching across all of India:** Not tied to a single factory; matches each product to India's specialist cities and hubs (**Kolkata, Kanpur, Agra, Chennai**, with quality oversight from **Maharashtra**) and splits processes by regional excellence when quality demands it.
- **Reviewed physical sampling and proactive pushback:** Weak samples are caught and discarded before the Client approves anything; even on strong samples, Alma proactively flags tiny refinements. KarmaRise guides Clients on what is needed for each dye batch and creates a physical colour swatch for Client approval, advising upfront on strap drop, grab handles, zippers, weight, sizing, and EU care tags.
- **Physical colour approval, NDA confidentiality, and written continuity:** Every call is followed by written meeting conclusions (`conclusiones de la reunión`) from Alma; mutual NDA/NNN confidentiality is available; physical swatches (not screen photos) lock colour expectations; production cutting starts only after the Client holds and approves the physical sample.
- **Human presence on the ground and zero-exploitation vetting:** Works with established family-run factories (`fábricas de corte familiar`), leather ateliers, garment makers, and women artisan collectives, walking away from secretive doors and speaking directly with artisans beside the stitching lines, not only owners.
- **Built-in craft credibility via AlmaRise D Brand:** Developing their own fashion and lifestyle label (`AlmaRise D Reborn`, `AlmaRise D Organic`, and `AlmaRise D Home Decor`) gives Alma and Shirish first-hand fabric, construction, and finishing experience in India rather than acting as detached middlemen.

**How we do it differently:** Instead of acting as a hands-off broker collecting a fee on an email handoff, KarmaRise D Global treats each engagement like a tailor-made garment: face-to-face video discovery, written Product Briefs, physical colour matching, in-person partner matching across India, sample pre-screening, live video updates from the cutting table, and legal/export accountability through delivery to the shop door.

**Why that's better:** Clients get better margins and bespoke products without taking on the operational, legal, or quality risk of navigating Indian manufacturing alone, and they stay connected to the real makers without flying to India for every revision.

**Why customers choose us:** Because "we have face and eyes" (not an anonymous platform), Alma speaks their language and understands European retail customers from experience, Shirish protects their interests on the ground in India with legal rigour, and every promise is verified on a physical sample before bulk money is cut into cloth or leather.

## Switching dynamics

**Push:** Pressure from easy online shopping squeezing physical retailers; selling the same wholesale products as neighbouring shops; poor communication, rising costs, or rigid MOQs with existing suppliers (including China); frustration with remote agents who promise everything and vanish when quality drops.

**Pull:** Launching a differentiated own-brand line with better margins and genuine craft; working directly in Spanish or English with founders who actually sit on Indian workshop floors; getting proactive design and ergonomic advice (straps, zippers, linings, sizing) instead of blind order-taking; transparent video updates from the factory floor.

**Habit:** Continuing to buy ready-made wholesale collections every season; staying with familiar Chinese or local suppliers out of inertia; post-COVID caution and fear of taking on a new overseas production workflow.

**Anxiety:** Fear of losing money or getting cheated in India; worry about language barriers, customs/export headaches, and late seasonal deliveries; uncertainty about starting without technical drawings or prior manufacturing experience.
