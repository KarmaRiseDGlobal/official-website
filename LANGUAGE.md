# Language, voice, and transcreation guide

KarmaRise D Global exists to tell a living human story between Spain and India. Every piece of writing across the website, Stories, newsletters, email templates, and UI microcopy must read like a warm, person-to-person conversation, never like corporate brochure text or mechanical translation placed into a layout.

## 1. Person-to-person conversation

- **Write from Alma and Shirish to one reader.** Picture speaking to a friend or collaborator across a worktable over coffee in Madrid or chai in Maharashtra.
- **Lead with warmth, curiosity, and excitement.** Let the joy of discovering fabrics, visiting workshops, and building things together come through in the rhythm of the sentences.
- **Invite dialogue.** Ask genuine questions and welcome replies, especially in newsletters and welcome notes.
- **Replace company jargon with lived scenes.** Instead of abstract business claims, name the people, the cloth, the city, and the moment at the table.

## 2. Story over UI filler

- **Every block must earn its place as part of the story.** Do not treat copy as filler text to occupy cards, grids, or hero banners.
- **Ground information in culture, customs, and fashion.** Connect practical details (pattern cutting, saree borders, leather hides, sample reviews) to the human customs, wardrobe habits, and regional traditions of Maharashtra and Europe.
- **Keep microcopy human.** Buttons, form labels, empty states, and confirmation messages should sound welcoming and clear rather than robotic.

## 3. Native transcreation, never word-for-word translation

- **Write each language from the inside out.** Whether writing in English, Spanish, or another language, compose for how native speakers actually talk, feel, and connect.
- **Never use literal machine-translation calques.** A sentence that works in English often sounds cold or awkward when translated word for word into Spanish. Do not mirror English sentence structure clause by clause. Rethink the thought from scratch and write it the way a native speaker would say it over coffee.
- **Spanish voice (`ES`):** Write in warm, colloquial, everyday Spanish (`español cercano y coloquial`, speaking `de tú a tú`). Use spontaneous, lived expressions (`¡Pasa, estás en tu casa!`, `Nos ha hecho una ilusión enorme`, `con un pie en España y otro en la India`, `sin rollos corporativos`, `Y oye, que esto no sea un monólogo`, `Un abrazo enorme`) rather than stiff corporate formulas or textbook translations.
- **English voice (`EN`):** Use warm, direct, conversational English with sensory detail and natural rhythm rather than corporate press-release language.

## 5. Pre-publish checklist

Before shipping any page, Story, newsletter issue, or email template in any language, verify:

1. **Person-to-person test:** Would Alma or Shirish actually say this out loud to someone they are excited to meet?
2. **Culture and craft test:** Does the text share a real detail about culture, customs, fashion, or the people behind the work rather than generic industry filler?
3. **Native fluency test:** Does the Spanish (or other target language) read as if it were conceived in that language first, with zero literal translation stiffness?
4. **Domain alignment:** Does the copy respect the core brand terms in `CONTEXT.md` while keeping the surrounding prose warm and conversational?
